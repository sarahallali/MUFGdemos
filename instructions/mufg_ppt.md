# MUFG PowerPoint Copilot Demo – Build Effective Presentations

**Scenario:** It's **Monday 9 November 2026**. The Executive Briefing on Project Meridian was approved on Friday: lead Fabrikam's €800m loan and keep **€250m** of it, subject to three conditions. On **Thursday 12 November**, Haruka Sato presents it to the EMEA Executive Committee in a 10-minute slot. You're helping her, and she needs a clear deck with speaker notes by tomorrow morning.

**Goal:** go from an approved document to a short, decision-focused presentation – and get ready to deliver it.

**Timing (60 minutes):** story so far 5 · Part 1 12 · Part 2 18 · Part 3 10 · five-minute challenge 7 · wrap-up 8

## Demo setup

## Copilot in PowerPoint

### Part 1 – Create

**1. Create the deck from the document**

In a blank presentation, open **Copilot** and choose **Create a presentation from a file** (or type **/** and pick `Meridian_Executive_Briefing_Final`). Add:

```text
Create a 10-slide presentation for the EMEA Executive Committee: title; decision required; executive summary;
the opportunity; transaction overview; financial profile; portfolio impact and hold options;
key risks and mitigants; compliance, ESG and operational readiness; recommendation and next steps.
```

**2. Check the storyline**

```text
Summarise this presentation in three key messages.
```

What to look for: (1) a strong opportunity with a long-standing client; (2) €250m keeps MUFG within its limits and, with the hedging and cash-management business, clears the 2.5% return hurdle; (3) approval comes with three conditions.

### Part 2 – Refine

**3. Add a comparison slide**

```text
Add a slide comparing the €200m, €250m and €300m hold options in a table: Industrials concentration including the pipeline,
single-name limit usage, and return on RWA including ancillary business.
```

**4. Add the decision slide**

```text
Add a slide titled 'Decisions required from the Executive Committee' that lists the decision and the three conditions.
```

**5. Make a busy slide concise**

[Slide: any text-heavy slide]

```text
Make this slide more concise for executives: no more than five bullets, each under twelve words.
```

**6. Better titles**

```text
Suggest a clearer title for each slide that states its key message.
```

**7. Visual ideas**

```text
Which slides would work better as a chart or visual? Suggest an option for each.
```

> **Check before you share:** compare every number on the slides with the source – see the [checklist](#appendix-numbers-to-check-on-the-slides). If Copilot rounds or drops a figure, fix it by hand.
>
> **Tip:** use Designer and your organisation's template for the look, and keep one message per slide.

### Part 3 – Get ready to present

**8. Speaker notes**

```text
Add speaker notes to each slide for a ten-minute presentation by Haruka Sato.
Use a confident, conversational tone and mention the one number to emphasise on each slide.
```

**9. Prepare for questions**

```text
What questions are Executive Committee members likely to ask about this proposal?
Suggest a short, factual answer to each, based only on this presentation.
```

Compare the answers with `Meridian_ExCo_Questions.docx`.

**10. Share the headlines**

```text
Summarise this presentation in five bullet points I can paste into an email to Tokyo Head Office.
```

> **Tip:** to practise the delivery, use **Slide Show → Rehearse with Coach**.

## Five-minute challenge

Haruka's slot has just been cut in half.

```text
Haruka's slot has been cut from ten minutes to five. Which four slides should she keep,
what should move to the appendix, and what should she say on each? Rewrite the speaker notes for a five-minute version.
```

## Try it on your own work

```text
Act as my Microsoft 365 Copilot coach. I work as a [role] in [department] at a bank in EMEA.
Suggest five ways I can use Copilot in PowerPoint for presentations I give. For each: a ready-to-use prompt,
the source document to start from, a follow-up prompt to improve the slides, and what I must check before presenting.
Start simple, then add two advanced ideas. Ask me questions if you need more context.
```

## Key takeaway

Copilot in PowerPoint turns an approved document into a first deck in minutes, then helps you sharpen the story, write speaker notes and prepare for questions. A person still checks every number and owns the message.
